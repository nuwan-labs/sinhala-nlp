4 · RQ2 — Segmentation & whether intrinsic measures predict bpb

Headline: whether Rényi efficiency predicts bits-per-byte beyond sequence length is design-dependent. The BPE-sweep design (a) is perfectly collinear (ρ(Rényi,len)=1.000) and cannot answer the question — the prior 'dissociation' was a design artefact. Across unit types (b) Rényi shows no signal beyond length; across algorithms at matched compression (c) it does. The proposal cannot claim a clean intrinsic/extrinsic (dis)association.

C2 — bits-per-byte (Kneser-Ney primary, fixed raw-UTF-8 denominator)

KN n-gram (order 3) bpb decreases monotonically across the BPE sweep; minimum within the swept range at vocab 8000 (bpb 0.590) — i.e. no interior optimum in [300, 8000]. By base unit, syllable (akshara) and large-vocab BPE compress best; byte is worst on bpb but (see C4) most robust. Word-level uses an explicit character-level KN backoff so every held-out byte receives probability (otherwise word-level bpb is not comparable).

Transformer (secondary): NOT executed. Capacity-controlled transformer training (fixed-dim factorised embedding / low-rank projection, total-param reporting, seed-spread vs config-spread check) was NOT run in this verification environment. The decision rule (usable only if config-spread > seed-spread and the trend is not explained by parameter count) is pre-registered; the neural number is left uncomputed rather than substituted. 12 · Plain-language verdict per RQ

RQ1. The data licenses the proposal to claim the register is restricted in concentration and class-separation: it is more lexically concentrated than the reachable general baseline on every metric (CIs separating), its indication constructions are sharply more concentrated than news (predicate-entropy gap far above SESOI), and its constituent/indication vocabularies are separated far beyond what layout predicts. It does not license the vocabulary-growth (Heaps' β) claim: across 12 unit×baseline cells none shows restriction at SESOI, and β often runs the other way because the constituent class is open. Confidence: high for concentration/separation (though resting on one reachable general baseline); the β claim should be dropped or inverted.

RQ2. The data does not license a clean claim that intrinsic measures predict (or fail to predict) bits-per-byte. The only design the prior work used — a BPE vocab sweep — is perfectly Rényi–length collinear and cannot answer the question. In a non-collinear design across unit types Rényi shows no signal beyond length; across algorithms at matched compression it does, but on only four points. The defensible statement is the dissociation is design-dependent and unproven; the deterministic KN bpb (minimum at the largest swept BPE vocab) and the robustness result are solid. Confidence: low for the predictive claim, high that design (a) cannot support it.

RQ3. The data (feasibility, distant labels) does not license the claim that register structure predicts extraction difficulty beyond coverage and layout: among out-of-list entities with field markers removed, the constituent−indication F-difference is equivalent-to-null and well-powered at this level. The tagger does recover unlisted entities far above a dictionary baseline (a positive extraction-feasibility result), and the open/closed concept asymmetry itself is real (hundreds of constituent concepts vs ~49 indication concepts, ~26% Sri-Lanka-specific unmapped). Confidence: the difficulty-asymmetry claim is unsupported pending the manual gold set; the structural asymmetry and extraction feasibility are supported.
